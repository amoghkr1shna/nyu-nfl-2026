Sharp cuts don't beat man coverage

Coaches call sharp-breaking routes "man beaters." We measured the cut on every route and found that sharp cuts gain no extra space against man. Man defenders take away about 0.3 yards more on every cut, sharp or soft.

How we measured it, and what to keep in mind
  • Break Sharpness: the receiver's largest change of direction over 0.5 s between snap and throw, times the share of speed he kept. A break only counts once he is moving at least 3 yd/s; without that, the stat picked up players standing still at the snap.
  • Space gained: distance to the coverage defender nearest at the start of the cut, measured again at the end, minus the starting distance.
  • Fair comparison: sharper cuts happen later in routes and start with the defender closer. Once those are held fixed, sharp cuts don't create extra space.
  • Most detected "breaks" are small adjustments: 81% of WR routes turn less than 30°.
  • The nearest defender stands in for the covering defender. Tracking ends about 0.5 s after the throw, so there's no separation at the catch.
  • All routes count, not only targeted ones, which keeps the quarterback's choices out of the measure.
